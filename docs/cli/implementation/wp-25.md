## WP-25 — word harvester: unknown words met in the deck's own examples

Status: planned 2026-10-24.

### Goal

The user's request (2026-10-24):

> Scan the cards to detect the words that are not in the deck and gather them for adding later, like a new word that appears in an existing example.

The user studies on a phone during the commute. Harvesting runs on the desktop; it feeds new cards to the phone deck. No step needs the user's attention except choosing which candidates to add.

### Where things stand

- After the WP-24 campaign, every vocabulary and grammar note holds structured content in the store: examples (sentence and translation), collocations, usage and nuance, each with provenance (`dictionary`, `generated`, `source`).
- `vocab add` (OP-21) prepares new notes from strict v2 documents (`--format jsonl`), and apply creates them (`create_note`).
- Japanese "is this the same word" logic exists: `head_word`, `dictionary_form` (する verbs, kana spellings), `same_spelling`. No tokenizer exists; nothing splits a sentence into words.
- English uses Cambridge; its pages list irregular forms (`went`, `gone`) that the parser does not read yet.

### Decisions

1. **Read from the store, not from Anki fields.** The scan reads the latest applied revision of each note's document (structured examples with provenance). Notes never revamped fall back to their Anki fields' visible text (`revamp::visible_text`). The scan is read-only for Anki.
2. **What is scanned.** Example sentences, collocations, and the sentences of usage and nuance. Meaning text is not scanned (it is the explanation language).
3. **Japanese tokenizer: Lindera with the embedded IPADIC dictionary** (Rust, offline, Apache-2.0; no download at run time). Each token gives surface, base form, reading and part of speech.
   - Dropped: particles (助詞), auxiliaries (助動詞), symbols, numbers, proper nouns (固有名詞), and single-kana tokens.
   - Compounds: IPADIC splits 交通費 into 交通 + 費. Adjacent noun tokens are merged when the merged form is a known word of the deck or an entry of the local JMdict file (decision 5); otherwise each part is its own candidate.
   - する verbs: a サ変 noun followed by する is the noun.
4. **English tokenizer: Unicode words plus a lemmatizer.** The lemmatizer applies built-in rules for plural `-s/-es/-ies`, `-ed`, `-ing` and doubled consonants, plus an irregular-forms table: built in, and extended by the irregular forms parsed from Cambridge pages already in the store. Function words come from a fixed stop list (about 300 words) and are dropped. Capitalized words in the middle of a sentence are treated as names and dropped.
5. **Optional local JMdict** (`harvest.jmdict_path`, EDRDG licence CC BY-SA 4.0, installed by the user). It confirms compounds and gives a reading and a JLPT level when Jisho is not asked. Without it, compounds stay split and ranking leaves out JLPT.
6. **Known words.** These count as known:
   - every expression of the purpose's decks, with `head_word`, `dictionary_form`, readings and the dictionary entry's forms (other spellings: 宜しく/よろしく);
   - split units;
   - words in the user's ignore list;
   - for English, the forms produced by the lemmatizer.
   A word known in another deck (the Japanese and English decks are separate) is still unknown for this purpose.
7. **A candidate** is one base form with:
   - its reading and part of speech;
   - the notes and sentences it was met in (context), with each sentence's translation and provenance;
   - its status: `new`, `queued`, `ignored`, `added` or `known`.
   Candidates live in the store (new tables, schema 16), so a rescan updates counts without losing decisions.
8. **Ranking**, highest first:
   - **i+1 sentences:** the number of the word's context sentences where it is the only unknown word. These make the best first example.
   - **Spread:** the number of distinct notes it appears in.
   - **Level:** its JLPT level when JMdict gives one, then its frequency.
   - **Provenance:** a word met in `dictionary` or `source` text ranks above one met only in `generated` text, since a generated example may use a rare word.
9. **Adding.**
   - `harvest queue` marks candidates.
   - `harvest export --status queued` writes v2 vocabulary JSONL: expression (base form), reading, and the best context sentence as the first example, with provenance `source` and a source note reference.
   - The ordinary `vocab add --format jsonl` and campaign-style batches then prepare the notes (dictionary, audio, generation, review) and apply creates them.
   - Exported candidates become `added` once their note exists.
10. **Incremental.** A rescan reads only documents whose revision changed since the last scan (a high-water mark per note). `harvest scan --full` rescans everything.
11. **Campaign safety.** The scan opens the store read-only for documents and writes only candidate tables. The schema 16 migration needs the store's exclusive lock, so it runs only while no campaign tick holds the lease (see steps).

### Commands

- `harvest scan --purpose P [--full] [--limit N]`: tokenizes and updates candidates. It prints a summary: notes read, sentences, new and updated candidates.
- `harvest list --purpose P [--status S] [--limit N] [--min-notes K]`: the ranked list. Each row shows the base form, reading, part of speech, scores and the best context sentence.
- `harvest show --purpose P WORD`: every context of one word.
- `harvest ignore|known|unignore --purpose P WORD...`: `known` means "I know it, no card". `ignore` means "not worth a card" (names the filter missed, slang).
- `harvest queue --purpose P WORD...` or `--top N`.
- `harvest export --purpose P --status queued --output FILE`: JSONL for `vocab add`.

### Steps

1. Tokenizer crate `linguist-text`: Lindera integration, compound merge, English lemmatizer and stop list, known-word matcher built on `head_word`, `dictionary_form` and `same_spelling`. This crate is shared with [WP-26](wp-26.md).
2. Cambridge parser: read irregular forms (`.irreg-infls .inf`) into entry metadata (`irregular_forms`).
3. Store schema 16: `harvest_candidates`, `harvest_contexts`, `harvest_marks` (scan high-water marks). Migration test from schema 15.
4. Application: scan (store reader, fallback to Anki field text), ranking, status changes, export.
5. CLI commands, JSON output, and docs in `operations/` (new OP numbers).
6. JMdict reader (optional path): forms, readings, JLPT tags, misc labels.
7. Tests:
   - tokenizer cases: 交通費, 勉強する, 食べさせられる → 食べる, kana-only words, names;
   - English lemma cases: running, studies, went → go, data;
   - the known-word matcher against `head_word` cases (費用(がかかる));
   - ranking;
   - incremental rescan;
   - export round trip through `vocab add` validation.
8. Live, read-only: run a scan of the user's revamped notes while the campaign runs, and report the top 50 candidates per language for the user to judge precision. Nothing is added without the user's choice.

### Done when

- `harvest scan` covers both vocabulary purposes and the grammar examples, and a rescan is incremental.
- On the user's collection, at least 90% of the top 100 Japanese and top 100 English candidates are real words not in the deck (the user's judgement on the list).
- Exported candidates prepare and apply as new notes through the ordinary path, with the context sentence as their first example.

### Environment and safety

- The scan reads only: no Anki writes, and no network unless Jisho or Cambridge is asked at export time.
- New notes are written only through `vocab add` and apply, under the existing checkpoint and verification rules.
- The schema migration needs the campaign paused: run `campaign wrap-up` first and never migrate while a tick runs.
- JMdict is installed by the user.
