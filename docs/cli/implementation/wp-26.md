## WP-26 — offline writing drills: tap, arrange and type, graded on the phone without an LLM

Status: planned 2026-10-24.

### Goal

The user's request (2026-10-24):

> I mostly study during my commute, on the train, on my phone, so I want a study process that is independent of an LLM. Voice recording is hard on the train, so we drop it. Decide on smart enough multiple-choice questions, arranging quizzes and other forms of test that improve writing skill (grammar, spelling, flow, natural sounding) for English, Japanese and more.

The reasoning behind it: good writing makes speaking easier, because the sentence already exists in the learner's head.

### Constraints

1. **Study happens in Anki on a phone** (AnkiDroid or AnkiMobile), offline. Grading is deterministic JavaScript in the card template: no network, no add-on, no LLM.
2. **Every drill is made at preparation time on the desktop** from verified material:
   - dictionary text;
   - the deck's reviewed examples;
   - rule tables.
   Drill generation calls no LLM, so it does not compete with the campaign's Codex quota.
3. **Nothing is spoken by the learner.** Listening with earphones is allowed: existing example readings (VOICEVOX, Piper, dictionary audio) can be played.
4. **Taps first, typing second.** Most drills are answered with one hand by tapping. Typing (phone IME) is kept for short answers.
5. **The vocabulary and grammar cards stay as they are.** Drills are separate notes on their own note type, so 18,000 revamped notes and their schedules are not touched.

### Decisions

1. **Note type `Linguist Drill v1`.**
   - Fields: `Kind`, `Language`, `Prompt` (HTML), `Data` (JSON), `Answer` (HTML), `Explanation` (HTML), `Source` (source note reference and word), `Audio` (optional `[sound:]` of the sentence).
   - One template, `Drill`. The front renders the drill from `Data`; the back shows the answer, the explanation and the source sentence with its translation.
   - Tags `drill::<kind>`, `drill::<language>` and `drill::src::<note id>`.
   - Drills go to a subdeck of the source deck (`森の言葉::Drills`, `Moonlit Manuscripts::Drills`, `夕暮れの詞::Drills`), so deck options (new cards per day, no autoplay) are set apart.
2. **Graded on the front.** A tap shows at once whether it was right, with the correct answer highlighted and a one-line reason. The learner then shows the back and rates the card (Again if wrong). Anki's JavaScript cannot rate a card, so the rating stays manual and honest.
3. **Variants without randomness that differs between sides.**
   - `Data` holds 1–5 variants of the same drill: for example, the same particle contrast in different sentences.
   - The front and the back choose the variant and shuffle the options with a seed made of the day number and a hash of `Prompt`. Both sides therefore agree, and the next day brings a different sentence, so the answer's position or sentence cannot be memorized.
   - No state is passed from front to back: `{{FrontSide}}` re-renders, and sessionStorage is not reliable on AnkiMobile.
4. **Which words get drills.** A drill is made only from a source note whose own card is mature: Comprehension interval ≥ 21 days, read through `cardsInfo`. Drills train using a word the learner already recognizes; they do not teach new words.
   - At most 3 drills per source note.
   - A source note's drills are generated again when its document changes.
5. **Distractors come from rule tables and the deck, never from guessing.** Each kind names its distractor source below. A distractor is dropped when the sentence it makes is found, verbatim, among the deck's examples or the dictionary's examples (it is then also correct).
6. **Weak spots steer the next round.**
   - `drills stats` reads the review log of drill cards (`cardsInfo`, `cardReviews`, read-only). It reports accuracy per kind and per confusion pair (は/が, に/で, in/on/at, make/do).
   - The next `drills generate` gives more variants to the pairs below 70% accuracy.
   - This adaptation is deterministic and runs on the desktop.
7. **Reporting a bad drill from the phone.** Flag the card (flag 3, green: "drill wrong"). `drills reported` lists flagged drill cards; `drills retire` suspends them, and the rule that made them is recorded as a known failure for the next generation.
8. **Language packs.** Each language supplies:
   - a tokenizer and chunker (from `linguist-text`);
   - confusion sets;
   - a conjugator;
   - a connector table;
   - a register table;
   - a misspelling rule table.
   Japanese and English ship first. Another language (for example Chinese or Korean) is added as a pack, with no change to the model or the template.

### Drill kinds

Each kind lists the skill it trains, how it is answered, where its sentence comes from, and how its distractors are made.

| Kind | Skill | Answer | Language |
|---|---|---|---|
| `arrange` | word order, flow | tap chunks in order | ja, en |
| `build` | sentence building from meaning | tap chunks, with decoy chunks | ja, en (when a translation exists) |
| `particle` / `function` | grammar | tap 1 of 3–4 | ja particles, en prepositions and articles |
| `form` | conjugation, spelling | tap 1 of 4, or type | ja, en |
| `spell` | spelling, kanji choice | tap 1 of 4 | ja homophones, en misspellings |
| `collocation` | natural sounding | tap 1 of 4 | ja, en |
| `connector` | flow, cohesion | tap 1 of 3–4 | ja, en |
| `error` | proofreading | tap the wrong chunk, then see the fix | ja, en |
| `register` | politeness, natural style | tap 1 of 3 | ja |
| `pattern` | grammar choice | tap 1 of 4 | ja grammar deck |
| `type` | writing a whole short sentence | type; Anki's built-in compare | ja, en |
| `dictation` | spelling from listening | listen, type | ja, en (sentences with audio) |

1. **`arrange`.**
   - A reviewed example is cut into 4–8 chunks. Japanese chunks are bunsetsu: a content word plus the particles and auxiliaries that follow it. English chunks are words, with punctuation attached.
   - The translation is the prompt. The learner taps the chunks in order, and the full order must equal the sentence.
   - Japanese: only sentences whose clause order is fixed by the language are chosen: the predicate last, at most one adverbial chunk, and no sentence where two noun-phrase chunks could swap. This keeps the one-answer rule fair.
2. **`build`.** Like `arrange`, plus 2 decoy chunks made by the `particle` and `form` rules (食べて against 食べで, `go to` against `go at`). It trains choosing as well as ordering.
3. **`particle` (ja) and `function` (en).**
   - One particle or preposition the tokenizer tags as a case particle or preposition is blanked.
   - The options are its confusion set:
     - Japanese: は/が, を/が, に/で, に/を, と/や, から/ので, まで/までに;
     - English: in/on/at, for/to, by/until, a/an/the/no article.
   - Pairs that are often both correct (に/へ, と/に with some verbs) are not drilled.
4. **`form`.**
   - The prompt is the dictionary form plus a target form ("て-form", "past negative polite", "passive"; "past participle", "third person -s"). The conjugator works from the part-of-speech class Jisho gives (Godan `ku`, Ichidan, irregular), and Cambridge's irregular forms supply English.
   - Distractors are the forms of the wrong class (帰て for 帰って, 行いて for 行って, `goed`, `runned`).
5. **`spell`.**
   - Japanese: the sentence shows the reading in place of the word. The options are the kanji spellings of other dictionary entries with the same reading (機会, 機械, 器械), taken from the store's Jisho pages or the local JMdict.
   - English: the correct spelling against misspellings made by rules:
     - doubled or single consonant (accommodate/acommodate);
     - ie/ei;
     - -ance/-ence, -able/-ible;
     - a dropped silent letter.
6. **`collocation`.**
   - A collocation from the note's verified collocations or dictionary examples, with its verb or adjective blanked.
   - The options are a light-verb confusion set:
     - English: make/do/take/have/get/give;
     - Japanese: する/やる/行う/取る/出す/付ける/掛ける, and 高い/大きい/強い for adjectives.
   - Only collocations whose blanked word belongs to a set are drilled.
7. **`connector`.**
   - A sentence (or two adjacent example sentences) holding a connector from the connector table, which sorts connectors into contrast, cause, addition, sequence and condition. The connector is blanked.
   - The options come from other classes only, so exactly one answer fits: しかし against だから against それに; however against therefore against moreover.
8. **`error`.**
   - A reviewed sentence gets one error injected by a `particle`, `form` or `function` rule.
   - The learner taps the chunk that is wrong, then the fix and the reason are shown.
   - Writing well includes reading one's own draft and catching this kind of slip.
9. **`register`.**
   - A sentence ending is shown in its plain, polite or humble form, made by a fixed table: る/ます, だ/です, いる/います/おります, 行く/行きます/参ります, 言う/言います/申します.
   - The learner chooses the one that fits the stated situation (a friend, a teacher, a customer).
10. **`pattern`.**
    - A grammar-deck example with its pattern blanked.
    - The options are other patterns of the same source page or lesson (Minna lesson tags; WP-22 units keep their page), so the patterns compete with their real neighbours: 〜ても against 〜ては against 〜たら.
11. **`type`.**
    - The translation is shown; the learner types the whole sentence. Anki's `{{type:Answer}}` compare shows the character errors.
    - Only short sentences are used (≤ 15 characters in Japanese, ≤ 8 words in English), because the compare accepts one answer only.
12. **`dictation`.** The example's existing audio plays; the learner types what they hear, and the same compare as `type` grades it. The audio is listened to, never recorded.

### Commands

- `drills generate --purpose P [--kinds K,...] [--limit N]`: a reviewable plan of drill items. Each item shows its rule, source sentence and distractors. The plan goes through the ordinary review and apply, so the agent or the user can spot-check it.
- `drills stats --purpose P`: accuracy per kind and per confusion pair (read-only).
- `drills reported --purpose P` and `drills retire NOTE...`.
- `models install drills`.

### Steps

1. `linguist-text` (from WP-25): bunsetsu chunker, English chunker, particle and preposition tagging.
2. Language packs ja and en: confusion sets, conjugator (Godan, Ichidan, する, 来る; English regular plus irregular), connector, register and misspelling tables, each with its own unit tests.
3. Core: the `Linguist Drill v1` model, the template (vanilla ES5 JavaScript, no external script, about 10 KB), CSS matching v3 and v4, and the drill document kind with its validation (answer among the options, distractors distinct, seeded variant choice).
4. Application:
   - drill generation from the store's reviewed documents and the maturity read from `cardsInfo`;
   - the verbatim-correct distractor filter;
   - weighting from stats.
5. The template tested in a headless browser (seeded choice equal on front and back; tap feedback; RTL and long-text layout at phone width), and on real AnkiDroid and AnkiMobile by the user (one test deck).
6. CLI, docs, desktop scenario (install the model, apply 10 drills, read back).
7. Live: generate 30 drills per kind from the user's mature notes into the Drills subdecks. The user studies them for a week of commutes and reports. `drills stats` and the flagged drills judge the rules.

### Done when

- All 12 kinds generate from the user's collection without an LLM call, and every generated item passes validation.
- The template works offline on the user's phone: taps, arranging and typed compare. Front and back show the same variant.
- After a week of use, flagged (wrong) drills are under 2% of drills reviewed, and `drills stats` reports accuracy per confusion pair.

### Environment and safety

- Drill notes are new notes in new subdecks, written by apply under the existing checkpoint rules. Vocabulary and grammar notes are not changed.
- Installing the new note type needs one full sync, and the user must choose **Upload**. Do it at the end of the WP-24 campaign, which already needs one.
- The phone is tested by the user; Anki is never started by the agent.

### Known limits — revisit after all packages

To be filled at completion. Expected:
- `arrange` and `type` accept one answer, while natural word order can vary.
- `particle` drills exclude pairs that are often both correct.
- Ratings stay manual.
- Free writing and its correction need an LLM and are out of scope for the phone.
